# 2026-11-04 — Family/pod communication drafting style (banked diffs)

Source: session drafting the pod teacher's holiday small-business message
(food stand vs art gallery, one initiative, two options). User iterated the
draft to a final shape, then asked for a diff analysis against the assistant's
version and said "bank those diffs for drafting work." These are the durable
drafting preferences and rubric extracted from that comparison.

## Context facts that shape the register
- The pod teacher is a **private employee of three families** — "risk of
  polite no" is zero. Power is never the lever; warmth buys authorship, not
  compliance. Workload is still a real design consideration (one person,
  scope discipline is the employers' job).
- Parent-side contributions are stated **collectively and as facts**
  ("the parents will provide frames"), never as personal offers ("I'd buy").
  Asymmetry to preserve: **commitments as facts, direction as invitation.**

## Vocabulary/tonal rules (learned corrections)
- Never "staffed" / resourcing-language for parent help → "available and
  happy to support," "happy to help hands-on," "glad to carry its share."
- Ideas-first openings: the high-level idea leads; the pod/classroom is the
  **center**, home is "support and supplement" (never the other way).
- Alternatives are presented as **options for one initiative**, not separate
  projects; always leave a third door ("or another idea altogether").
- Simplified register is a feature: short parallel objective bullets read as
  "manageable," which does quiet work against the workload concern.

## Structure that the user's final chose (conversation-shaped, not commission-shaped)
1. Idea (soft open: "we've been thinking it could be fun…")
2. Options A/B with the deadline pressure stated as *why we're raising it now*
3. Objectives, short parallel bullets, kid-level phrasing, one exclamation
4. Fun mechanics detail (recipes, voting, naming) — the delightful middle
5. **Help before asks** (labor offer precedes any request; "keep this small
   and manageable" lands workload evidence as reassurance)
6. Gentle asks, but the clock survives ([day] + "this week") — the part that
   actually prevents stall
7. Rough plan AFTER the meeting request, hedged as "one possible plan"
8. Closing line sets a shared success criterion ("fun for kids, workable for
   everyone") which implicitly licenses "then make it smaller"

## What the assistant's employer-shaped version kept that the final dropped
(flag: restore cheaply when it matters)
- "We want your version, not ours" / explicit decision-rights line. Cost of
  dropping: concrete mechanics read as expectation; invitee customizes only
  cosmetically. One-clause patch in user's register: "we're more interested
  in your version than ours, so push back on any of this."
- "Through experience rather than lessons about it" — the one sentence
  marking it as pedagogy, not a playdate.
- Hard weekend boundary ("no weekend time from you") vs soft ("parents would
  handle that part").

## Draft-quality rubric for this genre (from the diff analysis)
- Check the **commitments/directions asymmetry** (facts vs invitations).
- Check help-before-asks ordering.
- Check a clock with a named day survives the softening.
- Check decision rights are stated somewhere, or patch one clause in.
- Check collective parent voice throughout.
- Check workload evidence is retained but framed as reassurance, not lesson.
